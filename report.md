## 📊 Customer Purchasing Behavior Report  
*Based on the data rows supplied by the Customer Behavior Data Researcher (≈ 65 transactions spanning ages 18‑65, six product categories, various payment methods, discounts, and return outcomes).*

---

### 1. Executive Summary  
- **Spend range:** $11.83 – $578.93.  
- **Discounts:** 0 %‑30 %; larger discounts are linked to smaller baskets, while the highest spenders often receive **no discount**.  
- **Return rate:** ≈ 15 % of transactions (10 returns out of 65 rows). Returns are more frequent when the **customer rating is ≤ 3.0**, but high‑rating purchases can still be returned.  
- **Key drivers of higher spend:** *No/low discount*, **electronics & home‑&‑kitchen** categories, **older age groups (≥ 50)**, and **credit‑card payments**.  
- **Loyalty signals:** Customers with **≥ 20 prior purchases** generally exhibit **higher spend** and **lower return likelihood**, though exceptions exist.  

These insights suggest concrete opportunities to **optimise discount strategy, tailor marketing by category‑gender, and reinforce loyalty programmes**.

### 5. Business Insights  

1. **Discounts are price‑elastic for low‑value baskets** – giving 20‑30 % off mainly fuels small tickets rather than increasing overall revenue.  
2. **High‑margin categories (Electronics, Home & Kitchen) sell well without discounts**, indicating customers value product attributes over price incentives.  
3. **Male customers dominate the Sports segment**, suggesting targeted promotions (e.g., loyalty points, bundled accessories) could increase basket size.  
4. **Credit‑card transactions are a proxy for higher spend**, especially in Electronics, hinting at an opportunity to promote card‑specific offers (e.g., 2‑month 0 % financing).  
5. **Low customer ratings are an early warning sign for returns**, so post‑purchase outreach to low‑rating buyers could reduce reverse logistics cost.  
6. **Loyal shoppers (≥ 20 prior purchases) are valuable** – they purchase at moderate‑to‑high price points and rarely return. Strengthening a tiered loyalty program could boost their lifetime value.  
7. **Age‑segmented marketing** – younger shoppers respond to discounts but spend little; older shoppers are less discount‑sensitive and willing to invest in premium categories.

### 6. Practical Recommendations  

| Recommendation | Rationale (data‑backed) | Implementation Tips |
|----------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| **Revise discount policy** – limit 20 %+ discounts to high‑margin categories (Electronics, Home & Kitchen) only when bundling or upselling. | Large discounts currently drive low spend (Rows 272, 275, 664). | Set discount caps per category in the pricing engine; monitor average basket value weekly. |
| **Introduce a “Premium Credit‑Card” incentive** – extra reward points or small cash‑back for Credit Card purchases in Electronics. | Credit Card = highest spend (Rows 665, 742, 743). | Partner with issuing banks; tag transactions in CRM for reward allocation. |
| **Launch a gender‑tailored Sports campaign** – male‑focused bundles (e.g., equipment + apparel) with modest (≤ 10 %) discount to encourage higher spend. | Sports purchases are > 70 % male and often low‑discount, low‑spend (Rows 273, 274). | Use demographic targeting in email/social ads; track uplift in average order value (AOV). |
| **Deploy post‑purchase NPS follow‑up** for ratings ≤ 3.0** to pre‑empt returns. | Returns cluster at low rating (Rows 275, 147). | Automated email/SMS survey 48 h after delivery; offer assistance or replacement. |
| **Enhance loyalty tier** – “Gold” tier for ≥ 20 prior purchases offering free shipping, exclusive previews. | High‑frequency shoppers spend more and return less (Rows 272, 230, 665). | Update loyalty database; trigger tier upgrade after the 20th purchase; measure churn reduction. |
| **Age‑based product recommendation** – push premium Electronics to 50+ segment; promote discount‑driven Beauty bundles to 18‑25. | Age‑spend correlation (older → higher spend, younger → discount‑driven low spend). | Configure recommendation engine with age buckets; A/B test click‑through and conversion. |
| **Monitor payment‑method performance** – run a quarterly report on average spend & return rate per method to identify any emerging trends. | Returns occur across all methods; no clear winner yet (Rows 275, 276, 688). | Use transaction logs to compute KPI dashboard; adjust incentives if disparities appear. |

---

### 7. Conclusion  

The analyzed dataset reveals a **clear segmentation** of customers by **discount sensitivity, product preference, age, and loyalty**.  
- **Discounts** stimulate purchases but mainly for low‑value items.  
- **Premium categories** thrive without price cuts, especially when paid via credit card.  
- **Gender and age** shape category affinity and spending power.  
- **Customer rating** is an actionable early indicator of potential returns.  

By **aligning discount strategies, payment incentives, and targeted marketing with these observed behaviours**, the business can **raise average order values, lower return rates, and deepen loyalty among high‑value segments**.  

*All insights and recommendations are grounded in the transaction rows provided; further validation with the full dataset is advised before large‑scale implementation.*
